# Resume evidence

These bullets describe implemented, reproducible synthetic experiments — not live trading, historical
performance, or deployable profitability. Every number is regenerated by `corepack pnpm@9 run research:v2`
and stored in `benchmarks/research-v2.json`. Parts of the implementation were written with AI coding
assistance; the research design and claims are the author's.

## Current (V2) bullets

- Built a seeded options market-making laboratory in TypeScript where three quoting policies trade 1,000
  identical simulated sessions under quote latency, queue position, informed customer flow, and frictional
  delta hedging; on 200 held-out sessions the inventory- and toxicity-aware policy improved mean paired net
  P&L by **$72.91** versus symmetric unhedged quoting, 95% paired-bootstrap interval **[$48.56, $96.49]**.
- Attributed that improvement on development seeds instead of claiming it: frictional delta hedging cut
  losing sessions from **21.5% to 0.5%** and the worst session from **−$440.67 to −$1.87**, but a symmetric
  hedged quote of the same average width matched the adaptive policy (**−$1.48**, 95% CI
  **[−$4.62, $1.47]**), so the residual edge is spread width rather than directional quoting.
- Hardened the evidence pipeline: replaced a self-confirming P&L check with an execution-ledger
  reconciliation that rebuilds positions and cash independently, which exposed an accounting defect in
  sub-tick option liquidation; **49 automated tests**, a fail-closed evidence audit, and hostile stress
  regimes (35.5% losing sessions under extreme toxicity) that keep the project marked deployment-blocked.
- Implemented reproducible diagnostics for seeded GBM under P/Q measures, Black-Scholes Greeks, antithetic
  Monte Carlo intervals, put-call parity, and a discounted-price martingale check, served through a
  statically generated Next.js interface whose displayed numbers are read only from generated artifacts.

Do not claim the synthetic P&L as trading performance. Bootstrap intervals are deterministic resampling
diagnostics over simulated outcomes, not independent-market confidence intervals.
